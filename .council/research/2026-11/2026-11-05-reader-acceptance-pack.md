## Questions and method

Can readers identify the salary, fee and conditions relevant to a card;
retain their choices through a comparison; understand a salary-offer payout
sequence; and compare redemption options without confusing an editorial
valuation with a bookable fare? Test these decisions, not whether readers
like the colour palette.

Use five consenting UAE readers for an initial qualitative round. Include
both points beginners and experienced card users and more than one phone
type where available. This is a small diagnostic sample, not a survey,
representative conversion estimate or proof that all usability problems
have been found. Do not claim a session until an actual person completes it.

Allow about twenty minutes per session. Start at the homepage; give the
neutral scenario before mentioning a specific control. Observe first. If
help is necessary, record it rather than counting the task as unassisted.
Use fictional figures and no bank login, actual salary, account details or
application submission. Participation is voluntary; ask separately before
any screen recording and stop if private information appears. Default to
anonymous notes without recording or personal contact data.

The success criteria and route/control hints below are moderator-only
during an unassisted attempt. Give the reader the goal and fictional
scenario, not a checklist of the correct clicks; distinguish the owner's
directed device-acceptance round from neutral usability observation.

## Three neutral reader tasks

1. **Choose and compare.** You want cashback and earn a fictional
   AED 15,000 monthly salary. Find a possible card, identify its annual fee
   and VAT treatment, explain an eligibility condition, read its review
   and compare it with an alternative. Return to your original choices.
   Success: the reader can distinguish a shortlist from bank approval,
   find qualifying conditions and retain the same comparison basis.
2. **Understand a salary offer.** With a fictional salary in the
   AED 15,000–30,000 band, find a cash offer and explain what must happen
   before payment. Open the calculator without supplying an exact salary,
   then enter fictional AED 17,500.50 and clear it. Success: no band endpoint
   is mistaken for the reader's salary; cleared inputs do not leave an
   estimate; conditions/deadline/payment are understood as distinct.
3. **Use points.** From the Skywards guide, find the on-page redemption
   calculator, then explain how to compare a cash ticket with miles plus
   cash. No live fare is supplied or invented in the session. Success: the
   reader distinguishes a worked calculation, a programme baseline and
   the actual same-flight quote needed to decide. Identify what must be
   checked on the airline's website before moving any points.

After each task ask: “What did you expect?”, “What was unclear?” and
“How confident are you, and why?” Ask what they would do next and whether
there is a missing condition or explanation. Do not ask leading questions
such as “Was the new layout easier?”

## Triage and retest

- **Blocker:** inaccessible task/control, false fee/eligibility impression,
  lost input or a stale estimate presented as current. Fix and retest the
  exact route/state before release.
- **Major:** repeated difficulty finding conditions, interpreting a result
  or reaching the next step. Prioritise by task impact and recurrence.
- **Minor:** cosmetic preference without a demonstrated task problem.

Log the evidence, affected component, proposed smallest fix, owner and
retest result. Separate subjective preference from factual correctness.
Do not turn five outcomes into a site-wide success percentage. Use actual
findings to choose the next layout change rather than opening another
blanket redesign.
