A salary-transfer bonus is worth considering when the account suits you and
the conditions fit what you would do anyway. It is a poor reason to take a
card you do not want, borrow unnecessarily or commit savings you may need
elsewhere.

For UAE residents comparing offers, start with the [current salary-transfer
tracker](/salary-transfer/). Then ask a different question: **what must change
in my banking to earn and keep this reward?**

This guide compares those commitments, not personalised net returns. We have
not priced every account, card or finance cost, so we do not rank the banks
by an assumed net gain. This source-based research did not include an
application test or a payout verification.

## Check the route, not just the bank name

An existing customer may qualify for a campaign aimed at new salary
relationships, while someone opening a new account may still fail the
lookback test. Check the exact relationship the bank excludes: an account,
a previous salary credit, a card or a relationship elsewhere in its group.
Do not treat those as interchangeable.

The [Emirates Islamic campaign terms](https://www.emiratesislamic.ae/en/terms-and-conditions/salary-transfer-offer-terms-and-conditions-oct-2026)
illustrate the distinction. Existing and new account holders must enrol,
and the salary relationship must meet its group-wide eligibility test.
The account reward is separate from the optional Covered Card reward.
Opening an account alone does not complete the campaign.

Its exclusions matter too. The terms exclude salary credits into e-Savings
and Super Savings Skywards accounts, and customers with active Personal
Finance at payout. Choosing another product because it sounds similar—or
taking finance in pursuit of a larger banking bundle—could therefore work
against this particular offer. Check the actual account and campaign together.

## Decide whether the saving or spending commitment fits

First Abu Dhabi Bank's [salary campaign](https://www.bankfab.com/en-ae/personal/promotions/20-percent-cashback-on-salary-transfers)
offers a useful contrast. Its [conventional campaign terms](https://www.bankfab.com/-/media/fab-uds/personal/promotions/2026/20-percent-cashback-on-salary-transfers/terms-and-conditions-en.pdf)
allow qualification through saving **or** spending. A new conventional card
is required for the Spend route, not the Save route.

For saving, the test is an increase in the eligible month-end balance against
the previous month-end. Simply leaving an unchanged balance in the account
is not the same thing. For spending, the purchase must qualify under the
campaign's exclusions. Meeting both routes does not increase the cashback.

That makes the choice practical rather than promotional. Could you keep
building the required balance without stretching your cash needs? Or would
the eligible card spending already happen, with the bill paid in full?
Do not create extra purchases to make a reward calculation work.

The saving requirement is not itself a fee: the money remains a balance.
But it is still a commitment. Keep that separate from actual bank charges
and from the value you place on having the money available elsewhere.

## Keep bundled products out of an account-only comparison

Dubai Islamic Bank's published [XTRA terms](https://www.dib.ae/docs/default-source/pdf/dib-xtra-tc.pdf)
link salary transfer to an eligible Covered Card and/or finance facility.
That is a different decision from moving payroll for an account reward.
Its Home Finance table entry is an illustrative reversal of the actual
valuation fee charged, not a fixed cash joining reward.

We do not price those finance benefits here or assume an existing product
qualifies. The document's existing/new-product wording needs clarification,
and its hosted terms do not establish that new enrolment remains open.
Confirm both points with DIB before considering it. A possible fee reversal
does not show that a finance deal is cheaper overall.

## Price the account you will actually use

Make a short list of unavoidable charges: account maintenance, the selected
card's annual fee, transfers you regularly make and any cost of closing or
moving an existing relationship. Ask for the VAT-inclusive amount and the
conditions attached to any waiver.

Do not subtract a generic monthly fee if your chosen package waives it.
Equally, do not assume a salary campaign makes every service free.
Emirates Islamic's [account tariff](https://www.emiratesislamic.ae/en/soc/accounts)
excludes salary-transfer customers from its fall-below charge, but lists
other service charges separately. It states that its charges include VAT.
That is not a promise that every account or transaction costs nothing.

Keep **known cost**, **conditional waiver** and **not yet priced** as separate
entries. An unknown cost is not zero. If a required card or facility has not
been priced, a net-benefit verdict is premature.

## Separate earning the reward from receiving it

A campaign deadline is not a payday. Write down what completes qualification,
what starts the processing window and what must remain active at fulfilment.
Then record any continuing salary requirement that could reverse a payment.

FAB's terms describe monthly qualification and payment. A missed qualifying
month is not paid retrospectively, and closing the account before fulfilment
can forfeit unpaid cashback. Emirates Islamic separately provides for
disqualification or reversal under its campaign rules. Do not budget a
headline maximum as money already owed to you.

Our [payout-calendar guide](/news/uae-salary-switch-payout-calendar/) explains
the different clocks. Keep the bank's exact conditions beside your own
record rather than applying another campaign's timetable.

## Make the decision before changing payroll

Use the [salary-transfer calculator](/salary-transfer/calculator/) to explore
salary and product choices. Its estimates are conditional rewards, not an
approval check, guaranteed payout or complete switching-cost calculation.
If a card is compulsory, use [Card Finder](/cards/finder/) to assess it
separately; the bonus does not settle whether the card suits you.

If the account works without the bonus and the conditions fit, the reward may
be a useful extra. If you need an unwanted product, extra spending or an
uncomfortable savings commitment to qualify, leave the headline behind and
compare the underlying banking first.
