Most UAE landlords still want rent as one to four post-dated cheques.
A small group of companies now let you put that rent on a credit card
instead. They fall into **two very different kinds**, and mixing them up
is the most expensive mistake in this whole topic:

1. **Pay-by-card services** move your rent from your card to your
   landlord **on the schedule you already have**. If your lease says
   four cheques, you still pay four times — just by card. No loan, no
   fee to you.
2. **Monthly-plan services** ("rent now, pay later") pay your landlord
   on the landlord's schedule and let you pay **them** back in twelve
   monthly card payments. That is a form of credit, and you pay for it —
   **roughly 5% to 12% on top of your rent**.

If you only remember one line: **paying by card can be free; paying
monthly is not.**

## Option 1 — pay by card on your normal schedule (Tern)

**How it works.** You sign in with UAE Pass, upload your Ejari and add a
UAE credit card. On each rent due date Tern charges your card and sends
the exact amount to your landlord's IBAN. Your landlord does not have to
sign up or approve anything, though the name on the IBAN has to match the
tenancy contract.

**What it costs.** Tern says paying rent through it is free for you and
for the landlord, and that it makes its money from merchant partnerships
and its payments set-up. It does sell optional extras — it names
"Tern Split" — and says those fees are shown before you confirm.
Bayut, part of Dubizzle Group, which has invested in Tern,
describes the same thing: "no added fees or premiums for paying by card".

**What you earn.** Two layers. Your card's own rewards, plus Tern points
at **1 point for every AED 7** of rent, redeemable with partner merchants,
against a later rent payment, or as Air Arabia transfers. Tern does not
publish a fixed dirham value per point, so we do not convert it here.

**Who it suits.** Anyone whose landlord already accepts the number of
payments they can afford, who has the card limit to cover each one, and
who pays the card bill in full.

### The catches with Tern

- **It does not spread your rent.** If your lease says one cheque, Tern
  charges the whole year's rent to your card on one day. You need that
  much **available credit limit** on the due date. Tern charges at
  9:30 AM on the due date and sends reminders 7, 5, 3 and 1 day before.
- **Your existing cheques may be a blocker.** If you have already
  handed over post-dated cheques, Tern says some landlords will swap them
  mid-lease and others only at renewal.
- **Your bank decides the reward rate, not Tern.** Tern processes it as a
  normal purchase, not a cash advance, so cash-advance fees should not
  apply. But it also says "the exact reward category your bank assigns
  depends on their internal setup". Several UAE cards pay far less on
  rent-type spending — see the next section.
- **A failed payment is still a late payment.** Tern retries after 48
  hours and tells your landlord; any late fee is whatever your tenancy
  contract says.
- **Residential only.** No commercial leases, short-term lets or holiday
  homes — you need an Ejari or Tawtheeq contract.

## Option 2 — split your rent into twelve monthly payments

Here a company pays your landlord on the landlord's terms (say, one or
four cheques) and you pay the company back monthly. All three below say
in one form or another that this is "not a loan" because there is no
interest. **There is still a fee, and it is the cost of the credit.**

### Property Finder — Rent in 12

The only one of the three that publishes its full price list. The fee
depends on how many payments the landlord would normally take:

<div class="dp-data-table">

| Landlord's terms | Rent in 12 fee | On AED 100,000 rent you pay | Per month |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 cheque | 11.8% | AED 111,800 | AED 9,317 |
| 2 cheques | 8.00% | AED 108,000 | AED 9,000 |
| 3 cheques | 6.75% | AED 106,750 | AED 8,896 |
| 4 cheques | 5.75% | AED 105,750 | AED 8,813 |
| 6 cheques | 4.75% | AED 104,750 | AED 8,729 |

</div>

Fees are Property Finder's; the dirham columns are our arithmetic.

**Eligibility.** Monthly income of AED 8,500 or more, three months of
bank statements, a valid Emirates ID and a UAE-issued card. Only Dubai
residential homes listed on Property Finder at AED 30,000–400,000 a year,
and only through the Property Finder app. New arrivals and the
self-employed are assessed case by case.

**The fine print worth reading twice:**

- **Missed payment:** your saved card is retried daily for 30 days; after
  that Property Finder says "a civil case may be initiated".
- **Leaving early:** you pay the early-termination penalty agreed with
  the landlord "plus the applicable interest fees", and elsewhere the same
  page says you pay "any remaining monthly payments plus the remaining
  digital management fees". That sits awkwardly with the "no interest"
  description — ask for the exact exit cost in writing before you sign.
- **Credit score:** Property Finder says using the service does not
  affect your AECB score.

### Rently

Covers five emirates and says any landlord, agency or platform is
eligible, including mid-lease. Rently does not publish a percentage; it
says the fee depends only on the annual rent, the landlord's cheque
schedule and whether you also split your deposit.

**Rently's own worked example:** AED 90,000 rent normally paid as four
cheques of AED 22,500 becomes **AED 7,930 × 12**. That is **AED 95,160**
in total — **AED 5,160 more, about 5.7%** (our arithmetic), very close to
Property Finder's four-cheque fee.

**The catches:**

- **Deposit financing is an extra cost.** Rently will also split your
  security deposit (its example: AED 4,500 as AED 375 a month), and says
  the deposit option changes the fee.
- **Checks on you:** the application asks for a salary certificate and a
  credit report, and you need a valid work visa or a job offer.
- **Leaving early:** one month's notice and repayment of everything
  outstanding.

### Keyper

Keyper offers twelve monthly payments on a Visa card in Dubai and is
running a Visa prize draw. It does not publish a fee on its website, so
you only see the cost when you get a quote. Ask for the total you will
pay over the year, not just the monthly figure.

## Option 3 — Dubai's Flexi Rent (landlord-run)

On 23 June 2026 the Dubai Land Department launched **Flexi Rent** with
eleven property companies: Wasl Properties, Deyaar Property Management,
Dubai World Real Estate, Modern Real Estate, Dubai Investment Real
Estate, SBK Real Estate, Rocky Real Estate, SRG Properties, Harbor Real
Estate, Driven Properties and Al Showaib Real Estate.

These companies may offer monthly, quarterly or half-yearly payments on
**vacant or eligible units they own or manage**, sometimes with
incentives or discounts for new tenants, "in accordance with the
partners' approved policies". It is not a right every tenant can claim,
and DLD's announcement does not set a price or say which payment methods
must be accepted. If you rent from one of these companies, ask them
directly whether your unit qualifies, whether a monthly plan costs more
than paying by cheque, and whether they take cards.

## The hidden costs, in plain language

**1. The monthly-plan fee is the big one.** 5%–12% of a year's rent is
far more than any UAE card pays back in rewards. On AED 100,000 rent with
a four-cheque landlord, Rent in 12 costs AED 5,750. A card paying 1%
would return AED 1,000. You are paying for cash flow, not earning.

**2. Your card may pay almost nothing on rent.** Many cards have a
reduced rate for "real estate". Emirates NBD's own pages, for example,
list **0.2%** on real estate for the Visa Infinite, Visa Flexi, Visa
Platinum and Mastercard Platinum — on the Visa Infinite that is a tenth
of its 2% headline rate. Whether a rent payment lands in that bucket
depends on how your bank codes it; Tern itself says to call your bank if
you want certainty.

**3. Monthly or statement caps.** Many cards cap rewards per statement.
A single large rent payment can hit the cap in one go, so most of it
earns nothing.

**4. Card interest if you do not clear the bill.** Every option here
assumes you pay your card statement in full. If you carry the balance,
your card's monthly interest or profit rate (in its Key Facts Statement)
quickly costs more than any reward — and on a monthly plan, you would be
paying for credit twice.

**5. Your credit limit.** A large rent charge can use most of your limit
until the statement is paid, leaving little room for anything else that
month. If the limit is too low on the due date, the payment fails.

**6. Leaving early.** Monthly plans charge you the rest of the plan (and,
with Property Finder, remaining fees) on top of whatever your tenancy
contract says about early exit. Price the exit before the entry.

**7. Things that are easy to miss in a quote.** Ask each provider:

- Is the fee included in the monthly figure, and is VAT included?
- What is the **total** I pay over the year?
- What happens, and what does it cost, if a payment fails?
- What do I owe if I move out early?
- Does it cover the security deposit, and at what extra cost?

## Which one should you use?

- **You can afford your landlord's schedule and you clear your card in
  full:** a fee-free pay-by-card service is the only option here where
  rewards can come out ahead. Check your card's rate on rent first.
- **You genuinely need to pay monthly:** first ask your landlord for more
  cheques. Then compare the **total annual cost** of each monthly plan.
  More cheques from the landlord lowers the fee on every plan — on Rent
  in 12 it falls from 11.8% at one cheque to 4.75% at six.
- **Your landlord is a Flexi Rent company:** ask them first. A plan run by
  the landlord may avoid a third party's fee.
- **You do not clear your card balance each month:** do not put rent on
  it. The interest will outweigh everything above.
