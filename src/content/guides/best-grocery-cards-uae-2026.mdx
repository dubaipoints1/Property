Groceries are the one spend almost every UAE household has every week, and
the cards that pay most on them split into two kinds. **Store cards** pay
a high rate at one chain, in points you can only spend at that chain.
**Cashback cards** pay a lower rate at any supermarket, in money. Which
kind wins depends on where you shop, not on the headline rate.

This guide runs both kinds through two monthly grocery budgets and shows
the arithmetic. Every rate, cap, spend floor and fee comes from our card
database as verified on the dates shown on each linked review. Annual fees
are AED inclusive of 5% VAT.

## Store points against cashback

Three of the best rates in this guide pay in a currency that only works
in one place:

- **LuLu Points** redeem only at LuLu Hypermarket, LuLu Express and
  luluhypermarket.com. Our reviews value them at their published face
  value, 1 point = AED 0.01 (ADCB states it as 5,000 points = AED 50).
- **SHARE Points** redeem inside the Majid Al Futtaim network (Carrefour,
  City Centre malls, VOX). 10 points = AED 1.
- **noon Credits**, from the [Noon One](/cards/emirates-nbd-noon-one/), are
  not counted here: that card's accelerated rates apply on noon's own
  platforms (10% on NowNow, per our review), not at supermarket tills.

Store points are worth their face value only if you keep shopping at that
chain. If you would shop there anyway, the rate is the rate. If you split
the weekly shop between LuLu, Carrefour, Spinneys and the local Union
Coop, a cashback card that pays at all of them is usually worth more than
a high rate that applies to a third of your bill.

No card in our database publishes a Spinneys or Union Coop rate of its
own. At those chains you earn whatever the card pays on supermarkets in
general.

The arithmetic is the rate times the monthly spend, times twelve. For the
ADCB LuLu card at AED 3,000 a month: AED 3,000 × 8% = AED 240 of LuLu
Points a month, AED 2,880 a year. For FAB at AED 3,000: AED 3,000 × 5% =
AED 150 a month, exactly at its AED 150 category cap, so AED 1,800 a year.
Any FAB supermarket spend above AED 3,000 a month earns the 1% base rate,
per our review.

Four of these cards pay nothing at all in a month you miss their spend
floor, measured on **total** card spend, not just groceries:
[ADCB 365 Cashback](/cards/adcb-365-cashback/) needs AED 5,000 a month,
[HSBC Live+](/cards/hsbc-live-plus/) and
[DIB Consumer Cashback](/cards/dib-consumer-cashback/) AED 3,000, and
[FAB Cashback](/cards/fab-cashback/) AED 3,000 in the *previous* month.
The LuLu cards, the SHARE cards, ADIB and Citi have no floor in our data.

## Best for LuLu shoppers: ADCB LuLu Credit Card

The [ADCB LuLu Credit Card](/cards/adcb-lulu-platinum/) pays 8% back as
LuLu Points on LuLu purchases, with no minimum spend and no annual fee.
ADCB's own wording, recorded in our data, is that "purchases at LuLu are
not subject to the reduced earn rates applicable to the supermarket
category." Points redeem at any LuLu till in 5,000-point blocks worth
AED 50. Fuel earns 2 points per AED 1 and everything else 1 point. The
minimum salary is AED 8,000. Our data records no monthly cap on the LuLu
rate.

It beats the Emirates NBD LuLu 247 Platinum on our numbers. The Platinum
pays 7% at LuLu, needs an AED 12,000 salary, and costs AED 525 to join
and AED 262.50 a year, with no waiver published. At AED 3,000 a month at
LuLu, the ADCB card returns AED 2,880 a year against the Platinum's
AED 2,520, and the Platinum's fee takes that down to AED 2,257.50. The
Platinum's case is the rest of its earn table, 4% on fuel and 2% on
utilities, and worldwide lounge access through Mastercard Travel Pass.

**Earning under AED 8,000 a month?** The
[Emirates NBD LuLu 247 Titanium](/cards/emirates-nbd-lulu-247-titanium/)
pays 3.5% as LuLu Points at LuLu, free for life, from an AED 5,000 salary.
At AED 1,500 a month that is AED 52.50 a month, AED 630 a year. ADCB's own
entry card, the [LuLu Titanium Gold](/cards/adcb-lulu-titanium-gold/),
has no accelerated LuLu rate: our review records 1 point per AED 1 at
LuLu, the same as everywhere else.

## Best for Carrefour shoppers: Emirates NBD SHARE Visa Signature

The [SHARE Visa Signature](/cards/emirates-nbd-share-visa-signature/)
pays 6% back as SHARE Points at Carrefour, in store and online, and at
the other Majid Al Futtaim stores: City Centre malls, Mall of the
Emirates, VOX Cinemas, Magic Planet and Ski Dubai. It is free for life
and needs an AED 12,000 salary. At AED 1,500 a month at Carrefour that is
AED 90 a month, AED 1,080 a year.

The catch is at other supermarkets: groceries outside Carrefour earn
0.25%, and fuel and utilities 0.1%. Treat it as a Carrefour card, not a
grocery card.

The [SHARE Visa Infinite](/cards/emirates-nbd-share-visa-infinite/) pays
8% at the same stores, but its AED 1,575 annual fee only pays for itself
on Carrefour spend above about AED 1,640 a month (AED 1,575 ÷ 12 ÷ 8%).
It also charges an AED 1,500 joining fee, refunded if you spend AED 40,000
in the first three months, and needs an AED 30,000 salary. It earns its
place for a household whose mall spend, not just its grocery spend, sits
inside MAF.

## Best at any supermarket: FAB Cashback

For a household that shops at more than one chain,
[FAB Cashback](/cards/fab-cashback/) pays 5% at all supermarkets, in
cashback rather than store points (paid as FAB Rewards and redeemed in the
FAB app). The 5% is capped at AED 150 of cashback a month, which is
AED 3,000 of grocery spend. It also pays 5% on dining and fashion, under
separate AED 150 caps, within an AED 1,000 monthly total.

Net of its AED 315 fee, FAB returns AED 585 a year at AED 1,500 a month
and AED 1,485 at AED 3,000. Our review records a further AED 40 off a
Carrefour online order once a month on a spend of AED 250 or more. FAB's
first-year fee waiver ran with a welcome cycle that closed on 31 May
2026, and FAB publishes no spend-based waiver, so assume the fee posts in
full. The minimum salary is AED 5,000.

## Best with no spend floor: ADIB Cashback Visa

The [ADIB Cashback Visa Covered Card](/cards/adib-cashback-visa/) pays 4%
at supermarkets with no monthly spend minimum, capped at AED 300 of
cashback a month, which is AED 7,500 of grocery spend. Its fee is
AED 103.95. Net of the fee it returns AED 616.05 a year at AED 1,500 a
month and AED 1,336.05 at AED 3,000.

It is Sharia-compliant and also pays 4% on fuel, dining, school fees and
utilities, but nothing on anything else, including all international
spend. If your grocery bill swings from month to month, or your other
spend is small, this is the safer cashback card than FAB.

## Best uncapped: Citi Cash Back

[Citi Cash Back](/cards/citi-cashback/) pays 2% on groceries with no cap
and no spend floor, and 1% on everything else. The AED 315 fee is waived
from year two if you spend AED 9,000 a year, which AED 750 a month of
groceries alone clears. It is the card for a very large grocery bill:
above AED 7,500 a month, ADIB's cap binds and Citi's 2% keeps paying.
The minimum salary is AED 8,000.

## Before you apply

1. **Add up where you actually shop.** Look at three months of
   statements and split the grocery spend by chain. A store card's rate
   applies only to its share.
2. **Check the cap against your bill.** Divide the monthly cap by the
   rate: FAB stops paying 5% at AED 3,000 of groceries, DIB at about
   AED 6,667, ADIB at AED 7,500.
3. **Check the floor against your worst month.** A cashback card with a
   floor pays nothing in a month you miss it.
4. **Know what counts as a supermarket.** Banks classify merchants by the
   category the card network assigns them; ADCB's LuLu terms, for one,
   say so explicitly. If a store matters to you, check how it posts on
   your statement before counting on the bonus rate.

## Bottom line

If you shop mostly at LuLu, take the ADCB LuLu Credit Card: 8% at no
fee, paid in LuLu Points. If you shop mostly at Carrefour, the free SHARE
Visa Signature pays 6% in SHARE Points. If you spread the shop across
chains, FAB Cashback's 5% in cash is the best rate up to AED 3,000 a
month, ADIB's 4% is the safest with no spend floor, and Citi's uncapped
2% is the backstop for a large bill.
