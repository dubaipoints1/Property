Most newcomers budget for the car and forget the running costs. In the
UAE those costs are unusually front-loaded and unusually card-friendly:
almost everything — tolls, petrol, registration, insurance, servicing —
is paid by card, monthly or annually, in predictable amounts. That makes
motoring one of the easiest categories to optimise, and one of the most
commonly wasted.

This guide is part of the [DubaiPoints Expat Starter
track](/guides/expat-starter/).

<div class="dp-callout-tip">

**On prices.** UAE petrol prices are reset every month by the Fuel Price
Committee, and Salik and RTA fees change by decree. This guide therefore
does not quote them — anything printed here would be wrong within weeks.
It links to the authority for each instead, and concentrates on the part
that does not move monthly: what your card pays you back.

</div>

## The recurring costs, and how each is billed

| Cost | Billed | Where the current figure lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Salik** (road toll) | Per gate crossing, deducted from a prepaid balance | [salik.ae](https://www.salik.ae/) |
| **Petrol** | Per fill, price reset monthly | Retailer pumps; monthly rate set by the Fuel Price Committee |
| **Vehicle registration renewal** | Annual, with a mandatory test | [rta.ae](https://www.rta.ae/) |
| **Insurance** | Annual premium | Insurer |
| **Servicing** | Per service interval | Dealer or independent workshop |
| **Paint protection / detailing** | One-off, quoted per vehicle | Installer |

The first two are where card choice compounds, because they recur weekly
rather than annually.

## Fuel: the category where card choice actually pays

Fuel is the highest-frequency spend a car owner has, and several UAE
cards run an elevated rate on it. Every card below is a
[cashback card](/cards/cashback/). **The highest advertised rate is
usually the wrong card**, because every one of these rates is capped in
dirhams per month — and the cap, not the percentage, decides what you
actually collect.

<div class="dp-data-table">

| Card | Fuel rate | Monthly cap | Cap reached at | Annual fee |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Emirates Islamic Switch Cashback](/cards/emirates-islamic-switch-cashback/) | 8% | AED 100 | AED 1,250/mo | AED 313.95 |
| [HSBC Live+](/cards/hsbc-live-plus/) | 5% | AED 200 | AED 4,000/mo | AED 313.95 |
| [ADIB Cashback Visa](/cards/adib-cashback-visa/) | 4% | AED 300 | AED 7,500/mo | AED 103.95 |
| [DIB Consumer Cashback](/cards/dib-consumer-cashback/) | 3% | AED 100 | AED 3,333/mo | AED 210 |

</div>

Net annual return on fuel — cashback collected, minus the annual fee:

| Monthly fuel | Emirates Islamic | HSBC Live+ | ADIB | DIB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AED 800 | **AED 454** | AED 166 | AED 280 | AED 78 |
| AED 1,250 | **AED 886** | AED 436 | AED 496 | AED 240 |
| AED 2,000 | AED 886 | AED 886 | AED 856 | AED 510 |
| AED 3,000 | AED 886 | **AED 1,486** | AED 1,336 | AED 870 |
| AED 4,000 | AED 886 | **AED 2,086** | AED 1,816 | AED 990 |
| AED 6,000 | AED 886 | AED 2,086 | **AED 2,776** | AED 990 |

</div>

The card that wins changes twice. Below roughly **AED 2,000 a month** the
8% card is genuinely best. Between **AED 2,000 and AED 5,000**, HSBC
Live+ wins, because its cap is twice as high. Above roughly **AED 5,000**
the ADIB card wins on a 4% rate — half the headline of the top row — and
it is also the cheapest card of the four.

Fill a 60-litre tank weekly and you are comfortably past the first
crossover. The 8% card is a small-mileage card.

Three conditions to check before applying:

**Qualifying spend gates.** Emirates Islamic requires AED 2,500 of
monthly spend before the accelerated rate applies at all; HSBC requires
AED 3,000, below which everything earns 0.5%. DIB requires AED 3,000.

**The 8% is one mode, not a permanent rate.** Emirates Islamic Switch
lets you choose Lifestyle *or* Travel each month, and you earn the
accelerated rates of the chosen category only — not both.

**A conditional rate is not a rate.** The [Emirates NBD
Duo](/cards/emirates-nbd-duo/) advertises 5% on a set that includes fuel,
but requires AED 5,000 minimum monthly spend — below which it drops to
1.5% — and caps at 500 Plus Points per statement.

Annual fees above are as published; ADIB's is quoted VAT-exclusive, so
budget 5% on top.

<div class="dp-callout-tip">

**Salik top-ups do not always earn.** A top-up is a card transaction, but
it will not attract the fuel rate, and on two of the cards above it earns
nothing at all — ADIB Cashback Visa and DIB Consumer Cashback both pay
**0% outside their named categories**. DIB does list Salik and Nol
recharges as a category in their own right, capped at AED 25 a month.
Check the base rate before assuming a top-up is worth routing through a
particular card.

</div>

## Paint protection film: a one-off worth understanding before you buy

Paint protection film (PPF) is a clear or tinted urethane layer applied
over factory paint. In the Gulf the argument for it is specific: sustained
heat and UV, fine airborne sand that acts as a mild abrasive at speed, and
stone chips from construction traffic. It is a one-off cost rather than a
recurring one, which is why it belongs in a car budget rather than a
monthly one.

Coverage is sold in tiers, and the tier matters more than the brand:

<div class="dp-data-table">

| Tier | What it covers | When it makes sense |
|---|---|---|
| **Partial / bespoke** | Patch repairs, touch-ups, a single panel | Fixing damage, or protecting one vulnerable area |
| **Full front** | Bumper, bonnet, wing mirrors, headlights, wings, A-pillars | The panels that actually take stone chips |
| **Full vehicle** | Full front, plus doors, side skirts, rear bumper and rear quarter panels | Long ownership, or a colour that shows swirls |
| **Windshield film** | Glass only — a separate product | Chips and cracks, not paint |
| **Interior film** | Glossy trim and screens | Gloss-black consoles that scratch easily |

</div>

Finish is a separate choice from coverage. Clear film preserves the
original colour; satin ("stealth") and colour films change the appearance
while remaining reversible — relevant if you are leasing or plan to
resell.

Two things in their published material generalise to any installer you
evaluate.

- **Hand-cut versus machine-cut.** Modcare applies film hand-cut to the
  vehicle rather than using pre-cut patterns, and argues this avoids
  removing sensitive panels, avoids blades near the paint, and produces
  fewer visible edges. Whichever method an installer uses, ask which — it
  is the single biggest determinant of how the result looks up close.
- **Installation over brand.** Asked which film they use, Modcare's answer
  is that options including SunTek and STEK are discussed per vehicle, and
  that fit, clarity, heat performance and warranty support decide it
  rather than the label. That is the right answer, and an installer who
  leads with the film brand instead is selling you the label.

Like most installers in this category they do not publish prices, and
this guide quotes none — PPF is priced per vehicle, and any figure
printed here would be wrong for the car in front of you.

## What to actually do

1. **Work out your monthly fuel spend first, then pick the card.** It is
   the only input that matters, and it moves the answer between three
   different cards.
2. **Read the cap, not the headline.** On this data the 8% card returns
   less than the 4% card for anyone spending over about AED 5,000 a month
   on petrol, and costs three times as much in annual fee.
3. **Check the base rate before routing Salik through a card.** Two of
   the four cards here pay nothing outside their named categories.
4. **Treat PPF as a purchase decision, not a subscription.** Get quotes
   for a specific coverage tier, ask about the cutting method and the
   warranty behind the film, and judge the installer on edge work.
5. **Diarise the registration renewal.** It is annual, it requires a test,
   and the fine for lapsing is entirely avoidable.
