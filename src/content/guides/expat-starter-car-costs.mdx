Most newcomers budget for the car and forget the running costs. In the
UAE those costs are unusually front-loaded and unusually card-friendly:
almost everything — tolls, petrol, registration, insurance, servicing —
is paid by card, monthly or annually, in predictable amounts. That makes
motoring one of the easiest categories to optimise, and one of the most
commonly wasted.

## The recurring costs, and how each is billed

| Cost | Billed | Where the current figure lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Salik** (road toll) | Per gate crossing, deducted from a prepaid balance | [salik.ae](https://www.salik.ae/) |
| **Petrol** | Per fill, price reset monthly | Retailer pumps; monthly rate set by the Fuel Price Committee |
| **Vehicle registration renewal** | Annual, with a mandatory test | [rta.ae](https://www.rta.ae/) |
| **Insurance** | Annual premium | Insurer |
| **Servicing** | Per service interval | Dealer or independent workshop |
| **Paint protection / detailing** | One-off, quoted per vehicle | Installer |

The first two are where card choice compounds, because they recur weekly
rather than annually.

Two cautions before you apply for the top of that table.

**Cashback rates are usually capped.** An 8% headline is a monthly
maximum, not an unlimited rate — check the card's own terms for the cap
and the qualifying-spend condition before assuming the top row wins for
your mileage.

**A conditional rate is not the same as a rate.** The [Emirates NBD
Duo](/cards/emirates-nbd-duo/) advertises 5% on a category set that
includes fuel, but our data records that it requires AED 5,000 minimum
monthly spend — below which it drops to 1.5% — and caps at 500 Plus
Points per statement. That is a good card for a heavy spender and a poor
one for a light one.

<div class="dp-callout-tip">

**Salik top-ups usually earn too.** Salik is funded by topping up a
prepaid balance, and that top-up is a card transaction like any other. It
generally will not attract a fuel-category rate, but it does earn your
card's base rate — so routing it through the card with the best everyday
return is free money you are otherwise leaving behind.

</div>

## Paint protection film: a one-off worth understanding before you buy

Paint protection film (PPF) is a clear or tinted urethane layer applied
over factory paint. In the Gulf the argument for it is specific: sustained
heat and UV, fine airborne sand that acts as a mild abrasive at speed, and
stone chips from construction traffic. It is a one-off cost rather than a
recurring one, which is why it belongs in a car budget rather than a
monthly one.

Coverage is sold in tiers, and the tier matters more than the brand:

- **Partial / bespoke** — patch repairs, touch-ups, a single panel.
- **Full front** — the panels that take stone chips: bumper, bonnet,
  wing mirrors, headlights, fenders, A-pillars.
- **Full vehicle** — the above plus doors, side skirts, rear bumper and
  rear quarter panels.
- **Windshield film** — a separate product, aimed at chips and cracks.
- **Interior film** — for glossy trim and screens that swirl easily.

### A worked example: Modcare, Al Quoz

[Modcare](https://modcare.ae/) is a Dubai installer working out of Al Quoz
Industrial Area 2, offering the coverage tiers above along with detailing
and ceramic coating, window tinting, vinyl wrapping and styling. Their
[paint protection film page](https://modcare.ae/paint-protection-film/)
sets out the packages in detail.

What is worth understanding from how they describe their own work, because
it generalises to any installer you evaluate:

- **Hand-cut versus machine-cut.** Modcare applies film hand-cut to the
  vehicle rather than using pre-cut patterns, and argues this avoids
  removing sensitive panels, avoids blades near the paint, and produces
  fewer visible edges. Whichever method an installer uses, ask which — it
  is the single biggest determinant of how the result looks up close.
- **Installation over brand.** Asked which film they use, Modcare's answer
  is that options including SunTek and STEK are discussed per vehicle, and
  that fit, clarity, heat performance and warranty support decide it
  rather than the label. They make the same point we would: the film
  matters, but edge work, surface preparation and coverage decide the
  outcome.
- **Finishes change the car, not just protect it.** Clear film preserves
  the original colour; satin ("stealth") and colour films change the
  appearance while remaining reversible — relevant if you are leasing or
  plan to resell.

## What to actually do

1. **Pick the card before the first tank, not after.** Fuel recurs weekly;
   a two-point difference in rate compounds faster than any annual-fee
   saving.
2. **Read the cap, not the headline.** The highest advertised rate is
   frequently the most tightly capped.
3. **Route Salik top-ups through your best base-rate card.** It is a
   normal card transaction and most people never think about it.
4. **Treat PPF as a purchase decision, not a subscription.** Get quotes
   for a specific coverage tier, ask about the cutting method and the
   warranty behind the film, and judge the installer on edge work.
5. **Diarise the registration renewal.** It is annual, it requires a test,
   and the fine for lapsing is entirely avoidable.
