Family coming to Dubai and staying at a hotel without you? Start by checking what you actually have in your Marriott Bonvoy account: points or a Free Night Award. Then decide who should control the booking. Gifting a room and transferring points solve different problems.

## Gift the stay if you know the hotel and dates

If you have enough points and want to cover a particular stay, ask Marriott Bonvoy Member Support to book it for the guest. Have your membership details and the guest’s name ready. Already booked with points in your own name? The [programme rules](https://www.marriott.com/loyalty/terms/default.mi) say to ask Member Support to add the recipient, not the hotel.

Ask for a confirmation showing the guest’s name, the cancellation deadline and what happens if they need to change dates. The guest needs valid ID and a credit card or cash deposit for incidentals. Neither of you earns points or status credit on the gifted stay, and the guest does not get elite benefits from either account.

## Transfer points if the guest should make the booking

If your relative already has an eligible Bonvoy account and wants to choose or manage the stay, use Marriott’s [Share My Points](https://www.marriott.com/loyalty/earn.mi) route. That moves points you already own; the separate **Gift Points** option buys points. Check that both accounts qualify and that the recipient’s name and membership number match. Once the transfer is processed, the points belong to them.

Check the points price before transferring. For a Dubai weekend, compare it with the total cash price in AED for the same dates, room and cancellation terms. If the plan is still tentative, leaving the points where they are keeps your options open.

## A Free Night Award is different

A Free Night Award certificate cannot be transferred or used for a gifted stay where you are not staying. If that’s all you have, don’t count its face value as points you can send. Compare a paid booking instead, or save the certificate for a stay you’ll take yourself.
