A supplementary credit card can give someone in your household a card in their own name without giving them a separate credit line. Before they use it, check what the new card can spend, who is responsible for the bill and whose rewards balance receives eligible purchases. The bank’s limit and your household’s budget are not the same thing.

## Set the limit on the right card

[Emirates NBD](https://www.emiratesnbd.com/en/help-and-support/manage-your-credit-card-limits) says supplementary spending uses a share of the primary card’s credit limit. The primary cardholder chooses that share and can see the additional card’s transactions on the monthly statement. Check the amount the chosen percentage represents in AED, particularly if the main limit changes.

[HSBC UAE](https://www.hsbc.ae/ways-to-bank/mobile/card-services-faqs/) lets you set a monthly spending limit for each selected card. Limiting the primary card does not automatically limit the supplementary one. Choose the supplementary card in the app and check what the bank counts towards the cap: HSBC lists recurring transactions among the exclusions, while warning that some online or in-app subscriptions may still count.

## Check who owes the money and who gets the rewards

[HSBC UAE](https://www.hsbc.ae/credit-cards/products/supplementary-cards/) makes the primary cardholder responsible for amounts due on the additional card. The bank says qualifying supplementary spending contributes to the main cardholder’s rewards. [Emirates NBD](https://www.emiratesnbd.com/en/help-and-support/earning-plus-points) specifically says the Plus Points earned on a supplementary card go to the primary card. That is a Plus Points example, not a promise about every co-branded card or welcome offer.

Before you apply, read your card agreement and check the exact additional-card fee and any benefit you intend to use. Agree who pays for each purchase, set the limit on the new card, and check the first statement together. A household agreement can help you budget; it does not change what the bank can ask you to pay.
