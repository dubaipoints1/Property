Before you price your next UAE departure around an airline credit, check which credit you actually hold. A flydubai voucher follows the passenger named on it. Etihad Money sits in the holder’s Etihad Wallet and has its own expiry. Neither should be treated as cash you can hand to another traveller.

## If you hold a flydubai voucher

[The flydubai conditions of carriage](https://www.flydubai.com/en/information/policies/conditions-of-carriage/) say a voucher can pay only for the named passenger’s flights or optional extras. If a family booking has several names on it, don’t budget as though one voucher will pay everyone’s share.

The airline distinguishes the booking date from the flight date. Its conditions allow you to redeem before the voucher expires for an available future date. Find the expiry and voucher details before choosing flights, then check the remaining amount due before paying.

## If you hold Etihad Money or an old Etihad Credit voucher

Look in your Etihad Guest account for Etihad Money in your Wallet. [Etihad’s UAE Wallet help](https://www.etihad.com/en-ae/help/faq/manage) lists an **Add Voucher** option for an existing Etihad Credit voucher. Check the preferred currency before adding it; Etihad says this choice cannot be changed later. If you have several credits, check each expiry rather than relying on the total balance.

Use the Wallet at checkout on etihad.com for eligible flights or extras. Under [Etihad’s terms](https://www.etihad.com/en-ae/legal/terms-and-conditions), only the holder can use their Etihad Money and it cannot be transferred to another Wallet or cashed out. If you’re moving over an old voucher, check the expiry shown afterwards; don’t assume the move extends it.
