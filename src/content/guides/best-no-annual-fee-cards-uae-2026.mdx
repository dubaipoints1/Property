“No annual fee”, “first year free” and “fee waived after spending” are **different offers**. Compare the standard fee, the conditions that change it and any separate joining cost before treating a card as free to hold.

**Source review, 7 November 2026:** unsupported fee-waiver promises and annual-return examples have been removed. A missing fee row does not prove a fee is zero, and a current marketing headline does not resolve contradictory application terms.

## Read the fee label before the rewards

| Label | What it can establish | What still needs checking |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmed ongoing annual-fee exemption | The specified recurring fee is waived under the stated terms | Other charges and whether the exemption applies to your product or offer |
| First year free | The stated first-year fee concession | Joining costs and standard renewal |
| Renewal-spend waiver | A conditional route to avoiding the stated fee | Eligible spending, period, exclusions and whether the bank applies the waiver automatically |
| Limited-time application offer | A promotion for the defined application cohort | Offer dates, channel, customer category and conditions |
| Fee pending | The available evidence does not settle the cost | The current named product's fee guide and applicable terms |

“Free for life” is not a statement that borrowing, cash advances, overseas conversion or missed payments are free.

## Joining and renewal are not interchangeable

Where the issuer states joining and renewal charges separately, keep both in view. Do not assume the annual fee is the complete first-year cost, or add both charges without establishing when each is billed.

A nationality-specific or account-relationship concession is not a universal waiver. Likewise, an application headline should not be used to rewrite a standard renewal fee unless the applicable terms support that treatment.

Some current sources do not establish a distinct joining charge. That absence is not permission to fill a model with an invented zero.

## Check whether the rewards fit your spending

A card without an annual fee can still be a poor fit. Rewards may be limited to named merchants, subject to a spending gate, reduced for particular categories or paid as restricted platform credit.

Start with purchases you already make. Establish the eligible transactions and cap before estimating rewards. Keep a welcome promotion separate from the ongoing case for holding the card.

For account-linked products, confirm the account plan and its costs as well as the card fee. Do not describe the combined arrangement as cost-free merely because one card-charge field is zero.

<HotTip>
A pending cost is not cheaper than a confirmed one. If an essential fee or waiver condition is unresolved, ask the issuer rather than choosing the assumption that makes the card look free.
</HotTip>

## Before applying or renewing

Check the exact card name, current fee schedule, VAT wording and the offer attached to your application. If you rely on a spending waiver, confirm the eligible-spend definition and measurement period before moving purchases to the card.
