A renewed card can leave you with two surprises: a bill you wanted to keep may fail, while a subscription you meant to stop may carry on. One reason is that some merchants receive updated card details automatically and others do not. Don’t use the old card’s expiry date as a cancellation date.

## Check the payments you want to keep

[Standard Chartered UAE](https://www.sc.com/ae/important-information/abu-vau/) says Visa Account Updater can pass updated details for activated cards to participating merchants. [Citi UAE](https://www.citibank.ae/credit-cards/card-services/auto-billing-updater) says some merchants may update automatically but still tells customers to check after a renewal or replacement. That is not a guarantee for every biller or card.

Look back through your statements for insurance, telecoms and subscriptions, including annual renewals that might not be on your latest bill. For anything you want to keep, open the billing account and check the payment method before the next due date. Update it if needed, then check that the next payment actually went through. This checklist is for payments charged to a card, not debits from a bank account.

## Cancel the service with whoever bills you

If you want a subscription to stop, use the cancellation process of the company that bills you and keep the confirmation. [Dubai Islamic Bank’s Salam card terms](https://www.dib.ae/docs/default-source/cpr/tc/cards-tc-salam-cards-tc-en.pdf) make the distinction explicit: opting out of the Visa updater does not cancel your arrangement with a merchant. Before the next billing date, confirm that the service is marked as ending, rather than relying on a card change to stop the charge.
