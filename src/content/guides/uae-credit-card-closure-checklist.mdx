A card that no longer works isn’t necessarily a closed account. If you decide to cancel a UAE credit card, ask the bank to **close the account**, not just to block the card. The bank can stop new use while it works through amounts already due.

## Before you request closure

Check the latest statement and ask the bank what is still unbilled. [Emirates NBD’s cancellation guide](https://www.emiratesnbd.com/en/help-and-support/cancelling-your-credit-card) includes unbilled transactions and instalment plans in its closure checks. Ask how an active plan would be settled and whether an early settlement charge applies. If the card has a credit balance and the bank owes you money, ask how it will return it.

Review rewards before sending the request. Emirates NBD says unused rewards may be lost, subject to the programme’s rules. Check your card’s terms rather than assuming every points balance is treated the same. Tell anyone using a linked supplementary card and list the subscriptions you want to keep. Update or cancel those with each merchant: closing the card isn’t the same as ending a subscription.

## Keep the request and the closure separate

Save the bank’s acknowledgement or reference. The [Central Bank’s Consumer Protection Standards](https://centralbank.ae/media/ztidfnbr/notice-no-1457-2022-re-arabic-translated-consumer-protection-standards.pdf), clause 5.1.1.32, distinguish blocking a credit card account after a closure request from confirming its closure in writing. An unpaid balance can delay completion. If the card stops working or disappears from the app, don’t treat that as written confirmation.

Emirates NBD says delayed merchant transactions and other amounts already payable can appear after a cancellation request. If a charge posts, check what it relates to rather than assuming the block cancelled it. If closure is still pending, ask which transaction or balance is outstanding and what the bank needs from you. Keep the final confirmation alongside the request reference. Sources checked on 22 November.
