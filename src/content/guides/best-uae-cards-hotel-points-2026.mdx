A hotel-points card in the UAE means a Marriott Bonvoy card. That is the
short version of this guide, and it is worth stating before any table:
there is no UAE-issued [Hilton Honors](/hotels/hilton-honors/) co-brand,
and none for [Accor ALL](/hotels/accor-all/) that we track. If your
programme is Hilton or Accor, you earn it by staying, not by spending.

What the market does have is four Bonvoy cards from two banks. Two of them
are in our card database, reviewed and verified; two launched in late
August and are not yet. This guide sets them side by side on the same test
we use everywhere: what the card costs, what it earns per dirham, and
whether the welcome bonus pays for the fee.

Every card figure below comes from our card database as verified on the
dates shown on each linked review. Bonvoy points are valued at the
**2.5 fils per point** baseline set on the
[Marriott Bonvoy](/hotels/marriott-bonvoy/) programme page (AED 25 per
1,000 points). Earn rates are quoted by the banks per **USD 1**; we convert
at the 3.6725 peg.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| [ENBD Marriott Bonvoy World Elite](/cards/emirates-nbd-marriott-bonvoy-world-elite/) | AED 1,575 joining fee | AED 25,000 | **Required** | Up to 200,000 points | Gold Elite, 15 Elite Night Credits a year |

[Bonvoy programme page](/hotels/marriott-bonvoy/#how-to-earn-from-the-uae)
and our [launch report](/news/adib-marriott-bonvoy-cards-launch-uae/),
which also covers the lower-fee ADIB Visa Signature.

## What the earn rates are worth per dirham

Read the denominator first. "3 points per dollar" is 0.82 points per
dirham. At 2.5 fils a point, the ENBD pair returns:

| Spend | World (per USD 1) | World, % back | World Elite (per USD 1) | World Elite, % back |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marriott properties | 3 | ~2.0% | 6 | ~4.1% |
| Everything else, dining, international | 1.5 | ~1.0% | 3 | ~2.0% |
| Groceries | 0.375 | ~0.26% | 0.75 | ~0.51% |
| Fuel, utilities | 0.15 | ~0.10% | 0.3 | ~0.20% |

Two caveats sit in the small print of the card data. Spend in the **EU and
UK earns at 50%** of these rates. And both cards carry a **1.99%
foreign-currency fee**, which on the World card is larger than the ~1.0% it
earns abroad. On the World Elite the international earn (~2.0%) and the FX
fee (1.99%) cancel out. Neither is a card for spending overseas; both are
cards for spending at home and at Marriott.

## Best entry point: Emirates NBD Marriott Bonvoy World

**[Emirates NBD Marriott Bonvoy World Mastercard](/cards/emirates-nbd-marriott-bonvoy-world/)**
— AED 12,000 minimum salary, no salary transfer, FX fee 1.99%.

The case is the cost. ENBD currently advertises the card as free for life;
its standard Schedule of Charges lists an **AED 315** renewal, so treat the
zero as a campaign rather than a contract. For that, the holder gets
automatic Silver Elite status, 10 Elite Night Credits a year and a Free
Night Award each anniversary.

The welcome offer is two Free Night Awards of 35,000 points each on
**USD 6,000** (about AED 22,000) of retail spend across the first three
statements. At our baseline the two certificates are nominally
**AED 1,750** — but a certificate is capped, not cash. The Bonvoy page
notes that a 35,000-point night no longer reaches a peak-season Dubai
Marriott without a 15,000-point top-up, so spend them on an off-peak stay
or a cheaper property.

Hold it if you stay at Marriott a few nights a year and want status and
certificates for no fee. Think twice before making it your main spending
card: about 1% back on everyday spend, paid in a hotel currency, is a
modest return.

## Best for the big welcome: Emirates NBD Marriott Bonvoy World Elite

**[Emirates NBD Marriott Bonvoy World Elite Mastercard](/cards/emirates-nbd-marriott-bonvoy-world-elite/)**
— AED 25,000 minimum salary, salary transfer to ENBD required, FX fee
1.99%.

The welcome is up to **200,000 Bonvoy points**: 100,000 on activation and
payment of the joining fee, and 100,000 more on **USD 15,000** (about
AED 55,000) of retail spend in the first three statements. At 2.5 fils
that is **AED 5,000** of points against an **AED 1,575** joining fee.

Year two is the harder sum. The **AED 1,575** annual fee has no published
spend waiver, and the anniversary Free Night Award is capped at 50,000
points — **AED 1,250** at our baseline. The certificate alone does not
cover the fee; Gold Elite status and the ~4.1% earn at Marriott properties
have to make up the difference, and they only do if you stay.

The salary transfer is the other cost. Moving your salary to ENBD for a
hotel card is a decision about your bank, not about points — check what
the transfer is worth and what it commits you to on our
[salary-transfer tracker](/salary-transfer/) first.

## The ADIB alternative

ADIB's **Marriott Bonvoy Visa Infinite**, launched in late August as a
Sharia-compliant Covered Card, matches the World Elite's 200,000-point
welcome in two halves: 100,000 on issuance and a first retail transaction
within 30 days, and 100,000 for **USD 15,000** of retail spend in the first
90 days. The offer runs to **30 November 2026**.

Against the ENBD World Elite it trades a higher fee (**AED 2,500** a year)
for a higher hotel earn
(**8 points per USD 1** at Marriott Bonvoy hotels) and a larger anniversary
Free Night Award, valued at **85,000 points** (AED 2,125 at our baseline,
still short of the fee). ADIB does not publish the card's FX margin or a
minimum salary, and our [launch report](/news/adib-marriott-bonvoy-cards-launch-uae/)
explains why the FX figure is the one to get in writing before applying.

## Getting Bonvoy points from a bank programme instead

If you would rather keep a flexible bank currency, one route exists on
paper. **FAB Rewards** lists Marriott Bonvoy among its exits, alongside
Skywards, Etihad Guest and Avios, but FAB does not publish the ratio for
any of them. Our [transfer-ratios guide](/guides/uae-transfer-ratios-2026/)
explains the rule we apply: check the rate in the app at transfer time,
and if the implied value is worse than the cashback option on the same
balance, take the cashback.

Going the other way, Bonvoy transfers out to Skywards, Etihad Guest and
Qatar Privilege Club at 3:1 with a 5,000-mile bonus per 60,000 points.
That values a Bonvoy point at about 0.83 fils into Skywards or Etihad Guest,
a third of what we value it at on a hotel night. Earn Bonvoy to spend on
Marriott stays; do not earn it as a back door to miles.

## Hilton Honors and Accor ALL: no card to choose

Neither programme has a UAE co-brand, and none of the bank programmes in
our transfer-ratios guide lists either as an exit. Both are stay-led from
the UAE:

- **Hilton Honors** earns 10 points per USD 1 at most Hilton brands, with
  tier bonuses on top. We value the points at 1.5 fils, the lowest baseline
  of any programme we track. The only transfer route out, to Etihad Guest
  at 10:1, is almost never worth taking. See the
  [Hilton Honors page](/hotels/hilton-honors/).
- **Accor ALL** works as a fixed discount: 2,000 points take EUR 40 off any
  eligible booking. Points expire after 12 months without qualifying
  earning activity, which makes ALL the worst of the three to hoard (we
have not yet verified the expiry terms against Accor's own; confirm before
relying on them). See the
  [Accor ALL page](/hotels/accor-all/).

For either, the best card is simply the best card for your spending,
paid in cash or miles, with the hotel stay paid on it.

One hotel-adjacent card is worth naming, though it is not a hotel
programme: the
[Emirates NBD U by Emaar Infinite](/cards/emirates-nbd-u-by-emaar-infinite/)
pays 7.5% back as UPoints on Emaar spend, including stays at Address, Rove
and Vida hotels, and 20% off F&B and spas at Emaar Hospitality Group
hotels. It is an AED 1,575-a-year card, so it pays only if Emaar is
already where you spend.

## Our take

For most UAE readers who want hotel points, the
**ENBD Marriott Bonvoy World** is the starting point: no fee today, no
salary transfer, Silver status and a yearly certificate. Step up to the
**World Elite** or the **ADIB Infinite** only if you will clear USD 15,000
of spend in the first three months without forcing it, and only if you
stay at Marriott enough for Gold status to be worth something in year two.
Hilton and Accor loyalists should stop looking for a card and keep paying
for their stays with whatever card earns best.
