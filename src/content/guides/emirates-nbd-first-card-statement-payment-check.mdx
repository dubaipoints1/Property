The first bill on an Emirates NBD credit card can show a current balance, an amount due and a minimum payment. Start with the **latest statement**, not the largest figure in the app. The statement gives you a bill for a particular cycle; later spending can make today’s balance look different.

## Find the date and the two amounts

Look for the **payment due date**, the **total amount due** on the statement and the separate **minimum payment due**. The due date is when the bank needs the payment, not when it produced the statement. Emirates NBD’s [payment guide](https://www.emiratesnbd.com/en/help-and-support/manage-your-monthly-credit-card-payments) explains where to find both amounts and where to check payments you’ve made.

For ordinary card purchases, Emirates NBD’s [Key Facts Statement](https://www.emiratesnbd.com/en/kfs/credit-card) distinguishes a full statement payment by the due date from paying the minimum. The minimum is the amount needed to avoid the late-payment fee under the bank’s rules; it does not clear the bill. The bank may still apply finance charges if the statement balance is not paid in full.

## Check the payment after you send it

If you made a payment after the statement was issued, look at the payment history and ask the bank if the amount still due is unclear. Do not assume an instruction has already been credited. Keep the payment receipt until it shows on the card account.

If your statement includes a cash advance, a balance transfer or a special promotion, read the applicable terms separately. The Key Facts Statement excludes those categories from its standard retail interest-free treatment. Don’t use this guide to estimate what a mixed balance will cost.
