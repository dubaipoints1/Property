A starter card should be understandable before it is rewarding. Check **whether you qualify, what it costs to hold and how repayments work** before comparing a welcome offer or a headline earn rate.

Check the exact product rather than treating “starter card” as an eligibility rule. Some current products offer alternative salary or credit-limit routes, while other issuer documents conflict. Those qualifications remain explicit in the linked source profiles.

A pending salary field is not a lower threshold. A credit-limit qualification route is not a promise that the bank will grant that limit. Neither is equivalent to having no income requirement.

## Ask the issuer these questions

Before completing an application, ask which eligibility route applies to you and what supporting documents it requires. Confirm whether a salary transfer, an account relationship or an employer-specific condition applies; do not infer any of them from the card's tier or an incomplete comparison listing.

Ask the issuer to explain the joining fee, annual fee, applicable VAT and any waiver. “First year free” does not establish a free renewal. A renewal-spend waiver is a condition, not a fee-free product.

Also check the payment due date, minimum payment, applicable interest or profit charges, cash-advance costs and how missed payments are handled. Reward value should not be used to justify borrowing you cannot afford to repay.

## Keep the reward check simple

Choose a few regular purchase categories and confirm their actual treatment. Pay particular attention to bills, government payments, rent-related transactions, insurance and other reduced or excluded categories. Do not assume a general headline applies to them.

If the card uses points or platform credit, check where and how it can be redeemed. A captive reward is not automatically worth the same as cash. Treat welcome promotions separately from ongoing value, and verify that the current offer is open to your application channel and customer category.

<HotTip>
Start with the costs and conditions you can explain back in your own words. If a material term is unclear, ask the bank before applying rather than using a favourable assumption to complete the calculation.
</HotTip>
