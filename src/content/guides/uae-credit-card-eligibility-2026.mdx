A published minimum salary is **one condition, not an approval decision**. It does not by itself settle an employer-specific route, account relationship, salary-transfer requirement or the credit limit a bank might offer you.

**Source review, 7 November 2026:** the previous issuer-wide salary table has been withdrawn. Some current products describe alternative salary or credit-limit routes; other product and key-facts documents disagree. Those differences cannot be represented honestly by one universal number for each bank.

## Separate the questions

| Question | What to establish |
|---|---|
| Income route | The exact product's published minimum and any applicant-specific conditions |
| Alternative qualification | Whether the source offers another route, and how the issuer assesses it |
| Salary transfer | Whether your specific application requires it, rather than inferring it from a tier |
| Residency and employment | The conditions the issuer applies to your circumstances |
| Account or invitation | Whether a relationship or an invitation is required and currently available |
| Approval and limit | The bank's decision after considering your actual application |

An invitation-only description is not proof that new invitations are currently available. If an application route is unconfirmed, the profile remains available for reference but is not listed as open to new applicants.

## Do not infer the salary-transfer rule

A card's name, fee tier or income threshold does not establish a transfer requirement. Nor does a product page's silence prove that no transfer condition applies.

Where the current evidence is incomplete, we withhold the affected qualification claim rather than presenting an assumption as a confirmed rule. The source notes explain what still needs confirmation. This is different from changing a previously recorded “required” to a newly asserted “not required”.

Ask the issuer which rule applies to your specific product, application channel and customer category. If you are relying on a waiver or welcome offer, check whether its transfer condition differs from the basic application condition.

## Prepare questions, not assumptions

Ask what documents the issuer requires, what income or account relationship it will assess, and whether any employer, employment or residency condition applies to you. Confirm that the product is currently accepting your type of application.

If credit history or an assessment outcome matters to the application, obtain an explanation from the bank rather than treating a comparison website's salary filter as its credit decision. We do not promise that meeting a published threshold guarantees approval.

<HotTip>
“Minimum salary”, “salary transfer” and “approved credit limit” answer different questions. Keep them separate when comparing cards or discussing your application with the issuer.
</HotTip>

## Qualification comes before reward estimates

An attractive fee waiver, welcome award or reward rate is not useful if you do not qualify for the relevant offer. Confirm the exact customer category and channel before planning spending around a promotion.

Also check costs, repayment terms and whether the product fits your circumstances. An application decision and a rewards calculation are separate tasks.
