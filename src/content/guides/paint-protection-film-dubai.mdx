Paint protection film is the single largest discretionary spend most UAE
car owners will make on a vehicle they already own — and one of the least
transparent. Most installers quote per vehicle and publish nothing, so
the first quote you receive arrives with no frame of reference around it.

This guide builds that frame. A minority of Dubai installers do publish
prices, and their lists are enough to establish what the market actually
charges, what moves the number, and where the traps sit. It extends the
PPF section of our [UAE car running-costs
guide](/guides/expat-starter-car-costs/), which covers coverage tiers and
finishes in more depth.

<div class="dp-callout-tip">

**Why PPF is a Gulf-specific argument.** Sustained heat and UV, fine
airborne sand that behaves as a mild abrasive at speed, and stone chips
from construction traffic. Film is a physical sacrificial layer over the
factory paint; a ceramic coating adds gloss and easier washing but will
not stop a stone chip. The two are commonly layered — film first, coating
on top — and an installer who sells ceramic as chip protection is
mis-selling it.

</div>

**We have not used or tested PPS, Platinum One Auto or Desert Diamond.**
They appear here because they publish prices, which most of the market
does not, and published figures are the only ones a guide can honestly
quote. Treat the table as a price frame, not a shortlist.

## What moves a quote from AED 7,000 to AED 22,000

The table's spread is not noise. Four inputs drive it, and a quote that
doesn't itemise them is hiding something:

- **Vehicle class.** PPS prices by body type — a 4x4 costs AED 6,000 more
  than a coupé in the same film — because film is bought by the square
  metre and applied by the hour.
- **The film itself.** Platinum One's ladder runs AED 10,000 to 18,000 on
  the same car, purely by film: standard 8-mil films at the bottom, a
  ~10-mil flagship with a transferable warranty at the top.
- **Coverage.** Full front is roughly a third of the cost of full body on
  the published figures — and it covers the panels that actually take
  stone chips.
- **Time and environment.** Published install times run from 2 days
  (Platinum One, climate-controlled bay) to 7+ days (Desert Diamond's top
  package). A same-day full-body quote is a red flag dressed as a
  convenience.

## The warranty is two warranties — read both

The most useful thing in the published material is a distinction most
buyers never hear. Desert Diamond's price list separates the **film
warranty** (5, 7 or up to 12 years, backed by the manufacturer, covering
yellowing, cracking and delamination of the material) from the
**workmanship warranty** (1 to 3 years, backed by the installer, covering
the things that are actually most likely to go wrong: edge lift, bubbles,
trapped debris). A "10-year warranty" headline usually means the film
alone. Ask for both numbers.

Two related checks from Platinum One's published material generalise:

- **Warranty registration is the grey-market test.** Genuine film bought
  through authorised channels is registered with the manufacturer at
  install. Their advice — if a premium-brand quote looks too cheap, ask
  to see the warranty registration — is the cleanest single filter, and
  it costs you one question.
- **Transferability.** Some flagship films carry warranties that transfer
  to the next owner — worth actual money at resale time in a market
  where buyers discount unverifiable claims to zero.

Modcare does not publish prices — like most of the market, they quote per
vehicle — so the table above is your negotiating frame when you ask them,
or anyone, for a number. Their published material makes two arguments
that generalise to any installer you evaluate: film applied **hand-cut**
to the vehicle rather than from pre-cut patterns (no removal of sensitive
panels, no blades near paint, fewer visible edges — ask any installer
which method they use), and **installation over brand** (asked about
XPEL, their answer is that fit, clarity, heat performance and warranty
support decide the film, with options including SunTek and STEK discussed
per vehicle — the right answer; an installer who leads with the label is
selling you the label).

## The questions to ask any installer

1. **Hand-cut or pre-cut patterns?** The single biggest determinant of
   edge quality. Neither answer disqualifies, but the installer should
   defend theirs.
2. **Which film, and why this one for my car?** A per-vehicle answer
   beats a house brand recited from the wall.
3. **Is the film registered with the manufacturer at install?** The
   grey-market test. No registration, no premium price.
4. **What is the film warranty, and what is the workmanship warranty?**
   Two numbers. The second one is the one you'll use.
5. **Is the price ex-VAT, and what exactly is covered?** Both published
   price lists above quote ex-VAT; a verbal quote may not say. Get the
   panel list in writing.
6. **How long will you keep the car?** Two days in a controlled bay is
   normal for full body; same-day full body is a corner being cut.

## What to actually do

1. **Decide coverage before you collect quotes.** Full front covers the
   chip-taking panels at roughly a third of full-body money; full body is
   an ownership-length decision, not a styling one.
2. **Use the published figures as your frame.** Full front around
   AED 6,000–7,000 and full body from AED 7,000 to 22,000 depending on
   film, vehicle class and installer — a quote far outside that range
   needs a reason attached.
3. **Ask the six questions.** The answers matter more than the brand on
   the box, and the warranty-registration one takes ten seconds.
4. **Budget it in the car's one-off column.** PPF belongs beside the
   purchase, not in the monthly running costs — which is where our
   [car running-costs guide](/guides/expat-starter-car-costs/) picks up.
