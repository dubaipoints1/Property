Paint protection film is the single largest discretionary spend most UAE
car owners will make on a vehicle they already own — and it is a
custom-quote market. Installers price per vehicle: the make, the panel
list, the film chosen and the labour involved all move the number, so
two sensible quotes for two different cars can sit far apart and both be
fair. There is no meaningful sticker price, and this guide doesn't
pretend otherwise.

What you can control is quality. The result you live with for the next
five-plus years is decided by things you can ask about before paying —
above all *how the film is cut* — and that is what this guide covers. It
extends the PPF section of our [UAE car running-costs
guide](/guides/expat-starter-car-costs/), which covers coverage tiers and
finishes in more depth.

<div class="dp-callout-tip">

**Why PPF is a Gulf-specific argument.** Sustained heat and UV, fine
airborne sand that behaves as a mild abrasive at speed, and stone chips
from construction traffic. Film is a physical sacrificial layer over the
factory paint; a ceramic coating adds gloss and easier washing but will
not stop a stone chip. The two are commonly layered — film first, coating
on top — and an installer who sells ceramic as chip protection is
mis-selling it.

</div>

## Hand-cut or pre-cut: the question that decides the result

Every PPF job is one of two methods, and the difference is visible from
a metre away for as long as you own the car.

**Pre-cut patterns** — plotter- or laser-cut — are machine-cut from a
software template library before the film ever meets your car. The
pattern is deliberately sized slightly *inside* the panel, so the
installer never runs a blade near your paint and the job is faster and
more repeatable. The trade-off is built into the method: film that stops
short of the panel edge leaves an **exposed edge line** on every panel.
That line is where the compromise lives — it reads as a visible boundary
in raking light, it collects dirt and wax into a grey seam over time,
and a film edge sitting proud of the surface is exactly where peeling
and lifting start in Gulf heat.

**Hand-cut film** is fitted to your actual car, panel by panel, and the
edges are **wrapped around the panel** rather than stopped on its face.
Done well, the edges effectively disappear — there is no exposed seam to
catch dirt or begin lifting — and the film accommodates the small
differences between your car and the template ideal (aftermarket trim,
respray history, parking-sensor housings). The honest cost of the method
is that it depends entirely on the installer's skill: it is slower, it
demands blade discipline, and a careless hand-cut is worse than a good
pre-cut. Which is why *how* an installer hand-cuts matters as much as
whether they do.

This is the distinction behind our recommendation below. Modcare's
published position is that film should be bespoke and hand-cut rather
than pulled from pre-cut patterns — applied without removing sensitive
panels and without blades near the paint — precisely because of how the
edges finish. On the edge question we think they're right: a pre-cut
job's exposed edges are a permanent, visible compromise, and a properly
wrapped hand-cut edge is simply the better result. An installer offering
only plotter patterns isn't disqualified — but you should be able to see
the edge line in their own display work, and you should ask how it will
look on year three, not day one.

## The warranty is two warranties — read both

The most useful thing to know before comparing quotes is a distinction
most buyers never hear. A PPF job carries **two separate warranties**:

- The **film warranty** — backed by the film manufacturer, commonly
  quoted at ten years or more on premium lines — covers the material
  itself: yellowing, cracking, delamination.
- The **workmanship warranty** — backed by the installer, typically a
  year to three — covers the things most likely to actually go wrong:
  edge lift, bubbles, trapped debris.

A "10-year warranty" headline usually means the film alone. Ask for both
numbers, in writing. The second one is the one you are most likely to
use — and note that edge lift, the most common workmanship claim, is
exactly what the cutting method above is about.

Two related checks generalise to any installer:

- **Warranty registration is the grey-market test.** Genuine film bought
  through authorised channels is registered with the manufacturer at
  install. If a premium-brand quote looks too good, ask to see the
  warranty registration — the cleanest single filter, and it costs you
  one question.
- **Transferability.** Some flagship films carry warranties that transfer
  to the next owner — worth actual money at resale time in a market
  where buyers discount unverifiable claims to zero.

Modcare quotes per vehicle, like the rest of the market — the price is a
conversation about your car, not a list. Their published material makes
two arguments that generalise to any installer you evaluate: film
applied **hand-cut** to the vehicle rather than from pre-cut patterns

**installation over brand** (asked about film choice, their answer is
that fit, clarity, heat performance and warranty support decide it, with
options including SunTek and STEK discussed per vehicle — the right
answer; an installer who leads with the label is selling you the label).

## The questions to ask any installer

1. **Hand-cut or pre-cut patterns?** The single biggest determinant of
   edge quality. If pre-cut, ask to see the edge line on their display
   car — that is what your bonnet will look like.
2. **Which film, and why this one for my car?** A per-vehicle answer
   beats a house brand recited from the wall.
3. **Is the film registered with the manufacturer at install?** The
   grey-market test. No registration, no premium price.
4. **What is the film warranty, and what is the workmanship warranty?**
   Two numbers. The second one is the one you'll use.
5. **Is the quote ex-VAT, and what exactly is covered?** A verbal number
   may quietly exclude 5% VAT. Get the panel list in writing so quotes
   compare like for like.
6. **How long will you keep the car?** A couple of days in a controlled
   bay is normal for full body; same-day full body is a corner being
   cut.

## What to actually do

1. **Decide coverage before you collect quotes.** Full front covers the
   panels that actually take stone chips; full body is an
   ownership-length decision, not a styling one. Our [car running-costs
   guide](/guides/expat-starter-car-costs/) breaks down the tiers.
2. **Collect two or three quotes for the same panel list.** Quotes only
   compare if the coverage, film and VAT treatment match — fix those
   three and the differences that remain are real ones.
3. **Ask the six questions.** The answers matter more than the brand on
   the box; the cutting-method and warranty-registration ones take ten
   seconds each and tell you the most.
4. **Budget it in the car's one-off column.** PPF belongs beside the
   purchase, not in the monthly running costs — which is where our
   [car running-costs guide](/guides/expat-starter-car-costs/) picks up.
