Emirates is changing how Skywards Miles expiry is calculated. Miles earned
**from 1 June 2027** will be valid for three years from the earning date,
rounded to the end of that month. The rule they replace — three years,
then rounded forward to the end of your **birthday month** — is worth up
to an extra year of shelf life, and that is what is being removed.

Nothing changes for Miles you already hold, or earn between now and then.

## What the rule says today

Emirates' Programme Rules, clause 9, still carry the birthday-month
method. We checked the live page on 10 September 2026 and it reads:

> Skywards Miles will expire (and be deducted from a Member's Account) for
> each Member on the last day of the Member's birthday month that
> immediately follows the last day of the Validity Period ("Standard
> Expiry").

Emirates' own worked example in the same clause: a Validity Period ending
**15 January 2026** for a member whose birthday is **14 January** produces
a Standard Expiry of **31 January 2027**. The three-year clock ran out in
January 2026; the Miles survived until January 2027.

## Where the year goes

That example is close to the best case, and it is the reason the change
matters. Under the current rule the bonus depends entirely on where your
three-year anniversary falls relative to your birth month:

- **Anniversary lands just after your birth month ends** — you wait almost
  a full year for the next one. Up to roughly 12 extra months.
- **Anniversary lands inside your birth month** — days, not months.

Across a full year of birthdays the average sits near six extra months.
From 1 June 2027 that averages out at about two weeks, because the only
rounding left is to the end of the earning month.

At the **2 fils per Mile** cost basis this site uses for Skywards, a
50,000-Mile balance is **AED 1,000** of value with a shorter runway than
it used to have. That is the whole practical consequence: not fewer Miles,
less time to spend them.

## Who this does not affect

**Platinum members.** Per clause 6.10.1, Skywards Miles held by Platinum
members do not expire for as long as Platinum status is retained — an
Adjusted Expiry three months past the tier review date applies instead.
The earning-date change is a non-event at that tier.

**Anyone with a balance today.** The new method applies to Miles *earned*
from 1 June 2027. Existing balances and everything earned before that date
keep the birthday-month calculation, so there is no cliff edge and no
reason to redeem in a hurry.

## What we could not verify

Emirates has not amended the Programme Rules page to reflect the change.
The announcement was made in late August 2026 and **first reported by
[Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/business/aviation/emirates-changes-skywards-miles-expiry-rules-from-2027-1.500651718)
and Khaleej Times on 25 August**, both citing Emirates, including the
1 June 2027 effective date. The clause quoted above is what the binding
rules document said when we read it on 10 September 2026.

We will re-check the Programme Rules before the change takes effect. If
the amended clause lands with different wording than the press
descriptions — particularly on how partner-earned Miles are dated — that
is the version that governs, and we will update this piece.
