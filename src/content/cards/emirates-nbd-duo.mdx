The Emirates NBD Duo is the bank's dual-card product — a Diners Club and a Mastercard issued on
a single credit limit, with the earn assessed across the two cards together as Plus Points. The
pitch is network redundancy plus a category-bonus earn on grocery, fuel and utility spend; the
fine print is a spend gate, a points cap, and Abu Dhabi residency.

## What you get — per-benefit value

The Duo earns ENBD Plus Points. Their cash value depends on the redemption route;
statement cashback and selected non-cash redemptions have different published values.
Read the native-unit earning rows and redemption conditions together rather than
treating the headline as an unconditional cash return.

### Plus Points on qualifying categories

Use the named category rows, qualifying-spend threshold and combined statement cap
in the source-qualified earning table. Purchases outside those conditions do not
automatically receive the boosted rate.

### Buy-1-get-1 movie ticket at Royal Cinemas

A named benefit is not evidence of unlimited visits, guest admission or an unrestricted booking allowance. Use the current issuer/provider conditions for the qualifying spend, booking channel, fees and usage limit; unconfirmed allowances are not used for benefit matching.

### Airport lounge access

Lounge access is described by the issuer, but the applicable provider, visit allowance
and qualification conditions need confirming for the exact card. No cash value is
assigned to an unconfirmed allowance.

### Golf access

Complimentary access to top UAE courses per the issuer free-text — no round cap or
course list published, so we put no AED figure on it. Worth something to a golfer,
AED 0 to everyone else.

## Key conditions to know

<dl class="dp-conditions">
  <dt>Two cards, one account</dt>
  <dd>The product issues two physical cards — a Diners Club and a Mastercard — on a single credit limit and a single statement. Spending on either card draws on the same limit, and the Plus Points cap is assessed across the combined statement.</dd>

  <dt>Boosted earning — conditions</dt>
  <dd>Use the named categories and qualifying monthly-spend condition in the earning table. The points earned and their cash redemption value are different measures.</dd>

  <dt>Plus Points cap</dt>
  <dd>Read the combined statement cap in the source-qualified earning rows. The two cards do not create two independent reward caps.</dd>

  <dt>Abu Dhabi residency clause</dt>
  <dd>The Schedule of Charges note on this product reads "exclusive for Abu Dhabi residents only". Confirm with your ENBD branch whether this clause currently applies to your application.</dd>

  <dt>Foreign-currency fee</dt>
  <dd>The published foreign-currency percentage is the VAT-inclusive issuer fee, not an all-in conversion cost; scheme processing and any acquirer charges may be additional.</dd>

  <dt>Eligibility</dt>
  <dd>The issuer product page states a minimum monthly salary of AED 12,000. The reviewed product, Key Facts Statement and shared credit-card terms do not establish a universal salary-transfer requirement. Confirm the conditions of your own application; we do not assume a transfer-related clawback or that none can apply.</dd>
</dl>

## Watch out for

The foreign-currency fee shown is the issuer’s charge, not a total conversion-cost quote. The issuer fee guide states that scheme processing and any acquirer charges may be additional. A merchant’s AED-conversion offer can add a different cost; an issuer-fee waiver does not remove every possible overseas charge.

## Bottom line

An Abu Dhabi-resident card package whose boosted categories depend on qualifying monthly spend and a shared statement cap. It makes sense only if your real purchases fit those conditions and the benefits you can actually use justify the cost. Confirm pending qualification and benefit terms before applying.
