Al Hilal Bank is the ADCB Group's Sharia-compliant digital bank — founded
in Abu Dhabi in 2008, absorbed into the group in the 2019 three-way
consolidation with ADCB and Union National Bank, and since relaunched as
a digital-first Islamic proposition. The group plumbing shows in useful
ways: Al Hilal shares ADCB's offers platform and ATM network, so a
notionally branchless Islamic bank inherits one of the country's larger
cash-access footprints. Branch presence is essentially gone — the bank's
own site records the Ras Al Khaimah branch closing on 3 May — and the
relationship runs through the app. For the expat reader who wants
Sharia-compliant banking without DIB's or ADIB's branch-era product
sprawl, Al Hilal is the cleanest digital entry point in the market.

## The Cashback Credit Card, and why there is no spec table yet

Al Hilal's flagship consumer card is the
[Cashback Credit Card](https://www.alhilalbank.ae/en/personal/cards/credit-cards/cashback-credit-card),
a Mastercard upgraded in its current form effective 1 September 2024.
The design is genuinely distinctive. You **choose two of five
categories** — grocery, fuel, international, dining, education — and
those two earn the headline rate. That headline rate is then **tiered by
your total calendar-month spend**: 1% up to AED 3,000, 3% from AED
3,000.01 to 8,000, and 5% above AED 8,000, applied in your two chosen
categories. Everything else in retail earns a flat 1%; government
merchant codes and utility payments made in-app earn 0%; EEA
transactions always earn 1% whatever category they fall in.

The caps are where the arithmetic gets honest: **AED 250 per 5%
category** and **AED 500 total per statement cycle**. A two-category 5%
card that tops out at AED 500 a cycle is a solid mid-market cashback
product, not a premium one. There is no minimum spend to earn, you can
change your two categories once a month (free twice a year), and the
full MCC tables are published in the
[card terms](https://www.alhilalbank.ae/en/Images/ahb-cashback-tcs-final.pdf).

What we cannot yet show you is a fee table. The card's annual fee, FX
margin and any salary minimum are per the bank's
[Schedule of Fees](https://www.alhilalbank.ae/en/Images/Ahb-sof.pdf) —
a document that refused every fetch our tooling attempted. Until an
editor reads it, the card gets no entry in our tracker and this page
prints no fee figures. That is policy, not oversight: we publish numbers
we have sourced, and the SoF link above is where they live.

## Salary transfer: the campaign that ended, and still ranks

If you searched your way here for an Al Hilal salary-transfer bonus,
read this part carefully. The bank ran a genuinely large campaign — a
welcome package advertised at **up to AED 15,000**, built from a
salary-transfer component (10% of salary capped at AED 5,000, plus AED
5,000 more for transfers of AED 60,000+), card-spend tiers paying AED
500 to AED 3,000, and personal- and home-finance kickers. Its own terms
state: "The campaign will be valid from 4th May 2026 till 04th August
2026." **It has ended.** We checked the campaign page on 22 August 2026,
eighteen days after closing, and it still ranked first for the obvious
search queries — which is precisely how readers end up applying for
expired offers. Al Hilal is classified in our
[salary-transfer tracker](/salary-transfer/) as checked, campaign
expired 4 August 2026, with a watch on any successor. If a new campaign
launches, the tracker is where it will appear with verified bands.

Eligibility for the bank's products is assessed in-app under the usual
UAE rules: AECB reporting, the Central Bank's Debt Burden Ratio cap, and
Sharia-compliant contract structures in place of interest-bearing ones.

## Customer-service notes

Al Hilal is app-first, with [alhilalbank.ae](https://www.alhilalbank.ae)
as the public reference for products and fee documents. The ADCB Group
membership is the practical service story: shared ATM network and a
shared offers platform mean the digital-only footprint costs you less in
cash access than it would at a standalone neobank. Branch service has
been wound down to effectively nil, so weigh the app-only trade-off the
same way you would with Liv or Wio — faster routine servicing, chat
queues for anything unusual.

## Bottom line

Al Hilal suits the reader who wants Sharia-compliant banking in a modern
app, backed by ADCB's group infrastructure, and whose spend concentrates
in two of the five bonus categories heavily enough to reach the 3% or 5%
tiers — while accepting that AED 500 a cycle is the ceiling. It is the
wrong choice if you need published fee certainty today (the Schedule of
Fees is the document to read, and we haven't verified it yet), if you
want a live salary-transfer cash bonus — the AED 15,000 campaign ended
on 4 August 2026 whatever the search results imply — or if branch-counter
Islamic banking is the point, where DIB and ADIB still own the ground.
