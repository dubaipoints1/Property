Liv is Emirates NBD's digital-only brand — a standalone app, a standalone
card lineup, and a distinctly un-bank-like registration flow, all sitting
on ENBD's banking licence and ATM network. The Liv Key Facts Statement is
explicit that "Liv" is a brand owned by Emirates NBD Bank PJSC, which
matters for the expat reader in two ways: your deposits sit with one of
the UAE's two largest banks, and your service relationship runs entirely
through the Liv X app rather than a branch counter. There is no branch.
If that sentence bothers you, Liv is not your bank; if it doesn't, Liv
is one of the fastest routes to a functioning UAE account and credit
card, because application and approval are handled in-app end to end.

## Card ladder at a glance

Liv issues two credit cards to the expat market (a third, Tamayaz,
targets UAE Nationals and is outside our current coverage) — and,
unusually, you can hold only **one** of the two. Choose before applying.

## Reward currency, in plain English

Liv spend earns points, and the points have two exits: cashback against
the card, or **Emirates Skywards Miles** — up to 10,000 Miles a month on
the Cashback+ and 5,000 on the Cashback, with a 500-point minimum per
redemption. That Skywards route is the quietly interesting part of the
proposition. Most UAE entry-level cashback cards are a dead end for the
miles collector; Liv's are not, and because the brand sits inside ENBD —
Skywards' banking partner of longest standing — the conversion is a
first-party feature rather than a promotional bolt-on. The catch is the
tier mechanic: the advertised headline rates require serious monthly
spend, and government, utility and similar low-margin categories earn
0.1% whatever tier you reach. Read the card reviews for the arithmetic.

## Salary transfer and eligibility

Liv runs **rate-based** salary benefits, not the banded cash bonuses that
populate our [salary-transfer tracker](/salary-transfer/). The current
campaign pays 6% interest p.a. on savings in a Goal Account when your
salary lands with Liv, and the historic Bonus Multiplier programme paid
account interest scaled by salary band — in both cases the reward is a
deposit rate, not an upfront AED amount. That is why Liv appears in the
tracker's coverage note as checked-with-no-live-banded-offer rather than
as a row in the table: there is no cash figure to rank. For a reader
choosing where to land a salary purely for the joining bonus, RAKBANK or
the current tracker leader will beat a deposit rate; for a reader who
wants a low-friction digital account and will hold savings anyway, 6%
on a Goal Account is a genuine number worth checking against the
campaign terms in-app.

Liv assesses card applications in the Liv X app. UAE residency is required,
and meeting the published salary minimum does not guarantee approval.

## Customer-service notes

There is no phone-first service channel to recommend: Liv support runs
through in-app chat in the Liv X app, with [liv.me](https://www.liv.me)
as the public reference for rates, fees and terms. Cash access rides on
Emirates NBD's ATM network, which is as dense as UAE ATM coverage gets.
The practical trade-off is the same as every app-only bank: routine
servicing is faster than a branch visit, and anything unusual — a
disputed transaction, a document request — depends on chat queues.
As with all UAE issuers, AECB reporting and Central Bank Debt Burden
Ratio rules apply as standard.

## Bottom line

Liv suits the expat who wants ENBD's balance sheet without ENBD's
paperwork — an in-app account, cashback cards with published salary
requirements, and a Skywards transfer option. Take
the free-for-life Cashback card as a low-stakes first UAE card; take the
Cashback+ only if your total monthly spend genuinely clears the upper
tiers, because at the 1% floor its annual fee is hard to
justify. Look elsewhere if you want banded salary-transfer cash, branch
service, or headline rates that don't depend on how much you spent that
month.
